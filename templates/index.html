JobShield Protection

More Than Just a Fake Job Detector

JobShield combines several verification techniques to analyze different aspects of a job advertisement.

Website Verification

Checks the submitted job URL, website accessibility, HTTPS connection, redirects and final domain. Known company domains can also be compared with the submitted domain.

Risk Indicator Analysis

Rule-based analysis checks indicators such as application fees, sensitive information requests, unrealistic salaries, urgency, messaging platforms and suspicious recruitment language.

Machine Learning

A Logistic Regression model trained using the EMSCAD dataset analyzes job-related text and calculates the probability that an advertisement follows fraudulent patterns.

Risk Score

Rule-based indicators and the machine learning prediction are combined into a hybrid risk score ranging from 0 to 100.

Verification History

Previous job verification results are stored so users can review their analyzed jobs and risk information later.

Report Suspicious Jobs

Users can report suspicious job advertisements and provide additional information that can help identify potentially fraudulent listings.

Machine Learning

How the JobShield ML Model Works

The machine learning component learns statistical patterns from labeled job advertisements and uses those patterns to estimate fraudulent-job probability.

From Job Text to Fraud Probability

JobShield uses the Employment Scam Aegean Dataset, commonly referred to as EMSCAD, as the training dataset for its fake-job classification model.

The job-related text is converted into numerical features using TF-IDF. These features are then processed by a Logistic Regression classifier trained to distinguish between legitimate and fraudulent job advertisements.

Job advertisements are labeled as legitimate or fraudulent.
Text is converted into numerical TF-IDF features.
Logistic Regression learns patterns from the training data.
The trained model generates a fraudulent-job probability.
A 70% probability threshold is used by the current model.

Understanding the Risk Score

The hybrid risk score ranges from 0 to 100 and is converted into a risk category.

Low Risk

0 – 29

The available indicators produce a relatively low risk score and the advertisement is classified as likely genuine.

Suspicious

30 – 59

The advertisement contains indicators that require additional verification before proceeding.

High Risk

60 – 100

Multiple risk indicators and/or a strong ML fraudulent pattern contribute to a high risk score.
